# Classroom Practice: Evaluating Blog Credibility with the CRAAP Blog Analyzer

## Overview

This classroom practice helps students use the CRAAP Blog Analyzer to evaluate the credibility of an online blog post or news-style article. Students first make their own judgment using the CRAAP framework, then compare their reasoning with the tool's AI-generated analysis.

The goal is not to make the tool the final authority. The goal is to help students practice evidence-based evaluation, notice missing evidence, and explain whether a source is credible enough for a specific research purpose.

## Learning Goals

By the end of the activity, students should be able to:

- Explain the five CRAAP criteria: Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, and Purpose.
- Identify evidence that supports or weakens the credibility of an online source.
- Compare their own source evaluation with an AI-assisted evaluation.
- Verify at least one factual claim using an outside reputable source.
- Defend a final decision about whether a source should be cited for a stated purpose.

## Recommended Time

50 to 75 minutes.

## Materials

- Running CRAAP Blog Analyzer application.
- One assigned blog post, news article, or teacher-selected online source.
- Student worksheet: [student_worksheet.md](student_worksheet.md).
- Instructor guide: [instructor_guide.md](instructor_guide.md).

## Suggested Class Flow

### 1. Warm-Up: Source First Impressions - 5 minutes

Students open the assigned source and answer:

- What is the source mainly about?
- Who appears to be responsible for it?
- Would you trust it immediately? Why or why not?

Students should not use the AI analysis yet.

### 2. Establish the Research Need - 5 minutes

Students write a specific research question or information need. This is essential because Relevance depends on purpose.

Example:

> I need to decide whether this source is reliable enough to cite in a short school essay about renewable energy policy.

### 3. Individual CRAAP Evaluation - 15 to 20 minutes

Students complete the first pass of the worksheet:

- Currency: Is the source recent enough for the topic?
- Relevance: Does it fit the student's research need?
- Authority: Who wrote and published it?
- Accuracy: What claims need verification?
- Purpose: Is the source trying to inform, persuade, sell, entertain, or influence?

Students must write evidence from the article or from outside sources.

### 4. Use the CRAAP Blog Analyzer - 15 to 20 minutes

Students use the tool to inspect each CRAAP page:

- Currency
- Relevance
- Authority
- Accuracy
- Purpose

If the app is not in admin mode, students should load a saved analysis from the sidebar. If the app is in admin mode, the teacher can provide a URL for analysis.

Students should compare the tool's output with their own first-pass reasoning. They should mark where the tool helped, where it was incomplete, and where they disagree.

### 5. Fact Verification Mini-Task - 10 to 15 minutes

Each student or group chooses one factual claim from the source or from the tool's Accuracy page.

They must verify the claim using at least one reputable outside source, such as:

- Government or public agency website.
- Peer-reviewed research or academic publication.
- Established news organization.
- Recognized fact-checking organization.
- Publisher's original data, report, or primary document.

Students record whether the claim is confirmed, contradicted, incomplete, or not verifiable.

### 6. Final Judgment - 5 to 10 minutes

Students write a final decision:

- Credible enough to cite.
- Useful only as background.
- Not reliable enough for this purpose.
- Needs more verification before use.

Their conclusion must connect to the original research need.

## Student Deliverable

Students submit the completed [student_worksheet.md](student_worksheet.md), exported or copied into their class submission system.

Minimum expectations:

- Clear research question or information need.
- Evidence for all five CRAAP categories.
- At least one independently verified claim.
- Comparison between student judgment and AI output.
- Final source-use decision with justification.

## Assessment Criteria

| Criterion | Excellent | Developing | Needs Work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Research need | Specific and realistic | Present but broad | Missing or unclear |
| CRAAP evidence | Uses concrete evidence for all five criteria | Uses some evidence, but uneven | Mostly opinion without evidence |
| AI comparison | Identifies agreements, disagreements, and limitations | Mentions AI output but gives limited analysis | Accepts AI output without critique |
| Fact verification | Uses a reputable outside source and explains verdict | Attempts verification but source quality or verdict is weak | No meaningful verification |
| Final judgment | Clear, justified, and tied to purpose | Judgment is present but only partly justified | Judgment is unsupported |

## Extension Options

- Have groups evaluate different sources on the same topic and rank them by credibility.
- Ask students to compare CRAAP with SIFT or RADAR.
- Have students revise the tool's AI analysis by adding missing evidence or correcting weak reasoning.
- Run a short debate: "Should this source be cited in an academic assignment?"
